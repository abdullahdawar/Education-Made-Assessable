# Grade 1 Mathematics curriculum roadmap

Proposed Education Made Assessable sequence for Cambridge Primary Mathematics 0096 Stage 1. Chapter 1 already exists. Chapters 2–13 are planning proposals for educator review. The numbering belongs to this project. Teaching can interleave strands and revisit earlier skills, for example through daily calendar routines before Chapter 12.

The inventory uses the Cambridge-authored [Curriculum Framework v2.1, June 2025](https://studylib.net/doc/28550206/0096-mathematics-curriculum-framework--1-), Stage 1, pp. 17–19, viewed through a third-party copy. The objective summaries below are paraphrased. Cambridge's [0096 subject page](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/programmes-and-qualifications/cambridge-primary/curriculum/mathematics/) identifies three strands. Its [teaching guidance](https://cambridge-community.org.uk/guide-to/cambridge-primary/mathematics/) treats Thinking and Working Mathematically as integral to those strands and directs teachers to the support-site framework, progression grid and schemes of work. Confirm the edition and check the progression grid through the support site at educator signoff.

## Proposed chapter sequence

Lesson arrows show instructional order within a chapter. Assessment descriptions specify evidence to collect through observation, oral explanation, practical demonstration or written work. A digital selection alone can support practice without establishing the learner's method.

| Chapter and primary objectives | Prerequisite knowledge to check | Suggested lesson sequence | Teaching approach and interactive practice | Assessment expectations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Counting collections from 0 to 20** · 1Nc.01, 1Np.01 | Can match objects and attempt a short oral count. Scaffold from small sets. | Empty and small collections → larger organized and scattered sets → move the same objects and recount → check omissions and double counts. | Touch, move or mark each counter once. Use existing Counting Adventure and paired physical counters. | Count 0–20 across layouts, show a reliable counting method and justify an unchanged total after rearrangement. Observe the child's method separately from game scores. |
| **2. Number names, quick looks and estimates** · 1Nc.02, 1Nc.03, 1Ni.01 | Ch. 1 counting and counting to check. | Say, read and record numerals and number names → recognise familiar patterns through 10 → estimate unfamiliar collections or people through 20 → count to check. | Use familiar dice and frame patterns with learner-controlled reveal. Try estimate-then-reveal and numeral/name matching. Distinguish exact recognition from estimating. | Sample spoken, read and written forms across 0–20 and verify coverage across the package. Observe recognition without counting. Record an initial estimate and an exact check. |
| **3. Building, comparing and ordering numbers** · 1Np.02, 1Np.03, 1Np.04 | Chs. 1–2 totals, numerals and number words. | Build and break apart 10–20 → regroup the same amount in different ways → compare and order 0–20 → name positions to tenth. | Bundle and unbundle sticks, use frames and order picture cards. Try accessible build-and-exchange controls and direction-marked queues. | Show two valid groupings for a number and explain the same total. Order quantities from different layouts and distinguish how many from which position. |
| **4. Counting steps and object patterns** · 1Nc.04, 1Nc.05, 1Nc.06 | Chs. 2–3 number order and reading numerals. | Describe object sequences → step forwards and backwards → compare allowed step sizes → notice alternate even and odd numbers. | Walk a floor number track, then use tappable or keyboard-operated jumps. Describe a repeating unit aloud. | Start at varied values throughout 0–20 and stop at its boundaries. Count forward by 1, 2 or 10 and back by 1 or 10. Explain alternation and describe an object sequence. |
| **5. Joining, taking away and finding differences** · 1Ni.02, 1Ni.03 | Chs. 1, 3–4 cardinality, quantity comparisons and one-step counting. | Combine two groups → count on from a known total → take away and count back → compare two groups to find the difference. | Act out simple stories and pair objects to show an unmatched amount. Use a step-by-step counter activity with keyboard and touch controls. | Model both meanings of addition and all three meanings of subtraction. Match actions to simple representations and explain why a strategy fits a new story. |
| **6. Make ten, double and calculate** · 1Ni.04, 1Ni.05, 1Ni.06 | Chs. 3–5 partitions and operation meanings. | Complete ten → build doubles through double ten → estimate results → work out and check sums and differences with results in 0–20. | Pair counters or mirror a set. Use fill-the-frame and choose-a-strategy practice without time pressure. | Recognise complements of ten and know doubles through double ten. Give an estimate, compute and check in both operations, including boundary results. |
| **7. Equal parts and halves** · 1Nf.01–1Nf.04 | Chs. 1, 3 and 5 counting, comparing and partitioning. | Compare equal and unequal splits → find a half of a quantity or group → find half of a whole-number amount with a whole-number result → reassemble two halves. | Fold paper, share counters and rejoin pieces. Let learners choose split lines or distribute one object at a time. | Identify and explain equal and unequal splits, show a half of a group, find half within the required answer scope and reconstruct the whole. |
| **8. Recognising local money** · 1Nm.01 | Chs. 1–2 recognition of small quantities and numerals. | Recognise approved local coins and notes → match front and back → find examples in a pretend shop. | Use clearly distinguishable, accessible representations of locally relevant denominations. Try a matching activity. | Recognise a selection of local currency across different representations. Agree the locally relevant set with an educator. |
| **9. Flat shapes, solid shapes and turning** · 1Gg.01, 1Gg.03, 1Gg.06, 1Gg.07 | Can compare objects and describe simple features in familiar language. | Sort and describe flat shapes → handle and compare solid objects → distinguish flat shapes and solids → test when a turned shape looks the same. | Trace edges, handle solids and turn cards against an outline. Offer select-and-place or stepwise rotation controls. Shape Sorter is a development-only candidate for the 2D sorting lesson. | Explain a 2D sorting rule using sides or edge features. Describe and sort solids by faces, edges and flat or curved features. Distinguish 2D from 3D and show when a turn reproduces the same appearance. |
| **10. Position and directions** · 1Gp.01 | Can recognise familiar objects and use basic positional words. | Describe position relative to a reference → follow a direction → give and check a short route. | Walk floor routes or move a mascot marker one step at a time. Pair visual moves with spoken and text descriptions. | Place an object from instructions, describe where it is, and give a route that another learner can follow from the stated viewpoint. |
| **11. Comparing and measuring** · 1Gg.02, 1Gg.04, 1Gg.05, 1Gg.08 | Ch. 2 reads small numerals. Ch. 3 compares amounts. | Compare length, height and thickness → compare mass → compare capacity → explore numbered scales and choose tools for length, mass, capacity and temperature. | Align endpoints, use balances and safe pouring, and inspect rulers, scales, graduated containers and thermometers. Try tool matching and practical comparisons. | Use appropriate comparison language, show fair comparisons and choose a suitable numbered instrument for each attribute. Assess tool selection through objects and scenarios. |
| **12. Time, calendars and clocks** · 1Gt.01–1Gt.03 | Chs. 2–3 recognise numerals and order familiar events. | Describe time using familiar units → order days and months → connect daily events to clocks → recognise hour and half-hour times. | Talk through routines, arrange calendar cards and turn paired clock hands with buttons or manipulatives. Use locally familiar calendar terms approved by the educator. | Use meaningful time language, sequence days and months, and identify hour and half-hour times on varied clock faces. |
| **13. Sorting and representing data** · 1Ss.01–1Ss.03 | Chs. 1 and 3 count and compare. Ch. 9 uses sorting rules. | Answer a fixed question about categories → collect and record → organise and represent the same data in multiple forms → compare and discuss results. | Sort a shared collection, transfer objects to pictures, tables, diagrams and graphs, then inspect whether counts stay consistent. Use tables as accessible alternatives to charts. | Answer non-statistical questions. Across the package record and organise data with materials, drawings, lists, tables, Venn and Carroll diagrams, block graphs and pictograms. Describe and justify comparisons and conclusions. |

Number, geometry, time and data can recur across the year. Begin new chapters with a short prerequisite check and offer concrete scaffolds where needed. Place investigations and practice around school routines and use the [chapter blueprint](chapter-content-blueprint.md) for authoring.

### Thinking and Working Mathematically

These are recurring practices. The placements highlight observations to plan and record. Cambridge uses the characteristic names shown below in the [framework and teaching guidance](https://cambridge-community.org.uk/guide-to/cambridge-primary/mathematics/).

| Code and characteristic | Chapter evidence opportunities |
| --- | --- |
| TWM.01 · Specialising | Chs. 1, 7 · Try particular arrangements or splits to investigate a question. |
| TWM.02 · Generalising | Chs. 4, 6 · State a pattern that persists across cases and test it with another example. |
| TWM.03 · Conjecturing | Chs. 1, 9 · Predict whether the count or appearance will stay the same and test the prediction. |
| TWM.04 · Convincing | Chs. 1, 7, 13 · Use counters, drawings or data to explain and justify a claim. |
| TWM.05 · Characterising | Chs. 9, 11 · Name the properties that identify a shape or matter for a measurement. |
| TWM.06 · Classifying | Chs. 9, 13 · Sort by a stated property and explain group membership. |
| TWM.07 · Critiquing | Chs. 1, 13 · Evaluate a counting strategy or a claim made from a graph. |
| TWM.08 · Improving | Chs. 1, 10, 13 · Revise a count-check method, route instruction or data display after feedback. |

## Educator and language review before authoring

- Approve chapter boundaries and pacing, including whether to introduce shapes, measures and calendar routines earlier alongside number work. Confirm the mapping against the support-site progression grid and the framework edition used by the intended schools.
- Agree on which local coins and notes to recognise, the relevant calendar vocabulary, and contexts and objects children can access. Agree how much oral response and teacher observation is feasible in home and classroom settings.
- Confirm that Ch. 9 distinguishes recognising a tilted shape from identifying a turn that reproduces the same appearance. Approve the square–rectangle relationship and educator review of Shape Sorter before considering any learner placement.
- Review equivalent mathematical demand, acceptable answers and assessment evidence across both languages. A fluent Standard Written Pashto reviewer signs off on terminology, numerals, reading order and accessible labels. Existing material still requires the checks in the release checklist.
- Approve Ch. 13's treatment of non-statistical questions, the diagram progression and the number of representations expected across the chapter. Confirm that Ch. 11 teaches instrument selection for all four attributes.
