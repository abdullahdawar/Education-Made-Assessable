# Curriculum mapping for approval

Grade 1 maps to Cambridge Primary Stage 1, continuing one-to-one through Grade 6 and Stage 6. The catalogue has 39 grade–subject combinations: six subjects in Grades 1–3 and seven in Grades 4–6. Computing begins in Grade 4. Reserve two independently written examination sets per combination, for 78 sets in the full programme.

## Confirmed academic policy

Cambridge Primary is the primary framework for Mathematics (0096), English
(0058), Science (0097) and Computing (0059). Pakistan’s National Curriculum
is the primary academic reference for Urdu, Pakistan Studies and Islamiyat
in Grades 1–6. Use federal NCC publications as the national reference, and
record any applicable local implementation schedule separately.

The student-facing label remains **Pakistan Studies**. The baseline pathway
uses the social and citizenship outcomes of General Knowledge in Grades
1–3, Social Studies in Grades 4–5, and both History and Geography in Grade 6.
For the notified 2027–28 ICT and federal rollout, The World Around Us replaces
General Knowledge as the source for the Grades 1–3 pathway. Audit the science
outcomes in any integrated source against Cambridge Science before assigning
or cross-referencing them.

See the [objective register](curriculum-objective-register.md) for source
editions, implementation evidence, objective anchors and gaps in all 39
grade–subject combinations.

## Objective register and release review

Use one row per objective in the working register. Record framework edition, grade and subject, exact objective ID or page and row, a concise paraphrase, chapter, measurable outcome, worksheet or test item, and review status. An objective or item may appear in several chapters. Record where it is first taught, practised and assessed. Leave a visible gap wherever a source or mapping is unresolved. For each chapter, cross-check the expected outcomes against the register, including any skill that spans units.

The [counting sample](grade-1-mathematics-counting.md) illustrates this at chapter level. Once the authorised objective lists and edition-specific national outcomes are reconciled, reconcile each subject and grade against its full list and review the proposed chapter boundaries. Approve the final register only after every in-scope objective has a teaching location and suitable evidence, and every chapter has an objective and measurable outcomes.

Each chapter uses original, self-contained explanations, worked examples, independent practice and a separate answer key. Progress worksheets from guided practice (1–2), through independent application (3–5), to transfer and explanation (6–7). Test 1 checks core skills, Test 2 checks application and Test 3 checks transfer. Every test has a key and marking guidance. Each Mathematics and Science release needs equivalent English and Standard Written Pashto versions of the lesson and assessments, reviewed against the same objectives, task difficulty and scoring. Flag every Pashto lesson, worksheet, test, answer key and student-facing label for fluent-language review before publication. Use safe, accessible activities and offer paper alternatives where students may lack materials. Speaking, listening, recitation and practical performance need suitable evidence beyond written multiple-choice items. Narration and audio are outside this milestone.

Build subject examination Sets A and B from the same blueprint with different original items. Equalise objective coverage, marks, demand and duration across sets. Keep grade–subject exam coverage distinct from the three tests within each chapter. Provide keys and scoring guidance, with bilingual equivalence for Mathematics and Science. These are platform examinations.
